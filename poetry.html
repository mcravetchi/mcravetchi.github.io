VIII.

Phenomenology must be pursued for opening the true question concerning the method of philosophy. But I believe that such an aim is driven by an underlying insecurity in philosophy: first, the manifestation of instability in method itself; second, the urgency of supplying mere intuition with a structural augmentation; third, the persuasiveness of this endevour; fourth, that the flirtation of its theoretical completion as true positivism – all of this, is rather the after-effect of an underlining abyssal negativity, the out-pacing of a transcendental scarring whose mark had struck its founder and put him in a state of perpetual beginning. In other words – at a loss, played around with; someone whose every intention, however genuine, was subverted, whose every effort to reclarify left him further dependent on that very void he desired to fulfill.

— a note on my attitude towards phenomenology
