This is not a study of the Logical Investigations by Edmund Husserl. Rather – this is a radicalization of the phenomenological undertaking that begins by unwinding the tensions lingering in the very basis of its pronunciation. Overarching this effort is the gradual cultivation of an a-logical generative hermeneutic. This hermeneutic attempts to develop a manner of interpreting the correspondence between thinking and meaning. Branching out from phenomenology ensures that the possibilities opened up by phenomenology are sustained and intensified.

Motivation

Where do you think you are going?

‘Sidetracks’ bears the connotation of steering something towards that which is less important. They are diversions from a matter endowed with marked importance.

These sidetracks are devoted to such diversions.

For the connotation implies a logical structure. It encircles a discourse around that which has been ascertained as extant. It inculcates a pathos of proximity to the matter, a unidirectional threshold whereby distance corresponds to delusion. Most importantly, it slights the generative antecedent that institutes the systematicity of the scheme.

Philosophy is founded upon the acceptance of the logical scheme as a prime givens for the determination of entities. By way of this prime givens, he then projects this logical scheme onto states of affairs, whereby meanings are included or excluded with respect to his own theoretical grasping. Whether traversed, conquered, or revealed, manifolds are discovered by means of incursion.

Unlike the ilk of typical brotherhoods, philosophy goes great lengths in search of ways to temper this ineradicable seed of violence. It tries to teach how to eradicate the elements that prevent the grasping of logical reality – what and how it is for all. Since they originate from his own theoretical grasping, aberrations are said to originate from out of him. Such teaching is painful, as a fiction can, for instance, be added by the projector to prevent a confrontation with an otherwise deadly truth. To eliminate such fiction is congruent to an elimination of himself.

For such unreasons, not many men are attracted to the theater of mathematics, for it brings forth a manifold whereupon the scientists like to think that their objects do not so easily admit the spectacle of fantasies. Or rather – the manifold curates fantasies in which the grasping of entities upon that manifold are indubitable to all as long as they get used to the script. Not only does such getting-used-to inculcate the virtue of temperance, but it also shows how manifolds can be cultivated by playing rather than pillaging.

For Husserl, the casting of these roles is serious – it “demands a thoroughgoing division of labour here.”1 Such a division will be made as the difference between “essential insight” versus “scientific instinct.”2 Mathematicians specialize in the articulation of formal interconnections between mathematical objects. Philosophers, on the other hand, look towards the essence of a thing, an event, a theory.

Are manifolds however straightforwardly objectifiable? Are they not sensitive to the feeling of being thought of? How, then, is a “perfect, pure, genuine, theoretical knowledge” made synonymous with the philosopher’s “critique of knowledge?” in contradistinction to the mathematician’s “ars inventiva?”3 The manifold of philosophy makes it such that the philosopher seems to be threatened by the pointing of knowledge towards realms covered over. Concerned with its possibilities, it sensitizes the philosopher to delimitation. And only the philosopher has the right to say, ‘you cannot go there!,’ so that knowledge remains of him alone. According to another standpoint it is a form of self-soothing against pangs of inadequacy: ‘I feel that there is a truth in his words that I will not accept because I cannot go there myself.’ Moreover, the inventions of mathematicians are prone to naturalization, of becoming being. Instincts for something not yet in sight. Thus knowledge goes to the man who is meant to know. It is time to get sidetracked and see – where do you think your thinking is really going?

The decision of exploring with the Logical Investigations by Edmund Husserl is spurred by a conversion of the seminal thrust of phenomenology.

An accomplishment of metaphysical idealism is the realization that synthetic acts of cognition are later brought into the corresponding object as moments of its objectivity. This realization is logical because it is concerned with an underlying entity that is cognized by a cognition that knows itself to be cognizing this determinate entity. Deviating from this logical realization, the realization of phenomenology is epistemological. It is concerned with how acts of cognition give the entity as it is in the acts of cognition. Furthermore, it does not rest content with presuming the reality of these acts of thinking as would a psychology, but of indwelling in the sense endowed upon the essential generality of entities by any cognitive act whatsoever. While a metaphysical idealism tries to prove whether an act of synthesis actually inheres into the instantiation of an object, phenomenology emphasizes ‘life’ and ‘lived formulations,’ or the immanent description of how sense is given to entities as meant in this or that way. In other words, metaphysical idealism still has to prove whether the being of entities still belongs as determinately to this or that entity. Phenomenology, on the other hand, reflects upon the performance of the cognitive sequence through which the being of entities are given as they are found. The preliminary task of phenomenology, then, is to understand this phenomena of meaning.

To do so requires a linguistic conformism to the Idea of ‘Pure Logic.’ That is what the Logical Investigations is devoted to. To comprehend this, the Idea of ‘Pure Logic’ is stratified across three intentions: (1) to fixate categories of linguistic meanings to categories of logic, (2) to expose the laws and theories which have their grounds in these categories, and (3) to entertain a “pure theory of manifolds.”4 For Husserl, phenomenology is always phenomenology of perception. Knowledge is decreed as the “unity of objectivity… and truth.”5 Here, Husserl interprets truth as the interconnections of knowledge while objectivity is interpreted as the acts of truth which give that entity with respect to an already-established knowledge. Perceptively, any judgement of a state of affairs that is inwardly evident brings that state of affairs before our eyes. To reflect upon this judgement is to engage in an “ideational abstraction”6 through which the interconnections of the state of affairs are describable. But with science, there is an “extra-essential principle”7 wherein the field of science is brought into a constitutive mutual belongingness regarding common subject-matter. This entails that there are common acts of cognition that are performed in order to objectify that which science objectifies. As seen before, such technical inventions always presuppose the essential generality of the things in which they work around. If philosophy is to grasp these essential generalities and the acts of cognition which endow them sense, then there is a common theoretical matrix in which philosophers must come to perform common acts of cognition themselves. But these essential generalities are found to be in the knowledge of ‘pure’ logical laws. Thus, the way in which these acts of cognition are going to be objectified with respect to pure essential generalities is through an unnatural forceful adherence of the acts of thinking to the principial laws of logic. Only then can Husserl state that theory is an ‘all-embracing’ unity, a pure unity, one which begins with the critique of knowledge as the delimiting adherence of knowledge, and hence any and all acts of thinking, to pure laws of logic in order for knowledge to never lose sight of the essential generalities of things; of the things themselves.

Phenomenology describes the coming-into-knowledge, as coming-into a self-relation, of that which is sighted, that which is in-sight. Such is the seminal thrust that needs to be sidetracked. It is not without reason that phenomenology catches a glimpse of the theory of manifolds. In pursuit of this Idea of Pure Logic, the ground that is walked upon makes its heterogeneity heard. Yet these “fields of theory” are nevertheless glimpsed with respect to a construction “out of purely categorial concepts, many definite concepts” whose classes would “govern the legal connection, the transformation and the mutual interchange of these forms.”8 In a twist of dreams, this geopolitical thematic carries a detrimental ethos; the phenomenological residue of what is given to intuition, what is discovered by my in-sight, appropriated for my country. And this is not simply a rhetorical gesture – ‘Sidetracks’ is an-archic by philosophical necessity. The very imposition of a legal classification that mediates the transformations and interchanges between manifolds inculcates a technical-political motif that assumes the phenomenologist-king can get behind the totality of the manifold as though it were an object, as though it can get behind it logically without traversing the material. Such is a false way to describe how to approach manifolds.

Keywords of the Generative Hermeneutic (I)

Phenomenology begins with its own extra-essential act. The common schemata of the placement of technics in service of language in service of thought is found in the proposition. Between the mathematical discipline and the philosopher lies a gulf as the difference between a “growing system of propositions having a naively factual validity” and the

“essence of the modes of cognition which come into play in their utterance and in the ideal possibility of applying such propositions, together with all such conferments of sense and objective validities as are essentially constituted therein.”9

Conferment of sense and objective validities refer back to the material concatenations designating the scientificity of any science versus any other. What these sciences do not render transparent to themselves is the field of significance that modes of cognitions pass through in their production. But in this distinguishing example, the system of propositions is provided as available, as the ‘possibility of applying such propositions:’ the applicability of these propositions are ideally circumscribed and hence present. Therefore, significance is interpolated with respect to signification. The primary disposition of a subject is to signify. And out of the system of knowledge relevant to a certain objectivity, it is language which traverses all of them and is that out of which signification can occur.

The Idea of Pure Logic must be instituted into the field of knowledge that is language – to ensure that the Ideas of logic are constantly operational in the acts of thinking; to ensure that the description of the modes of cognition are reduced to the logical and therefore essential signification of any perceived generality. Phenomenology will do so by rendering “self-evident in fully-fledged intuitions that what is here given in actually performed abstractions is what the word-meanings in our expression of the law really and truly stand for.”10 As elements of propositions, that a word is involved in a proposition formed in the reflection of a cognitive performance indicates an inflection of its concept, such that “by distinguishing among concepts confounded by us, and by suitably modifying our terminology, we then likewise achieve a desired ‘clearness and distinctness’ for our logical propositions.”11 Between these two statements stands a complicity between completeness of intuition and confoundedness of conceptuality. The plenitude of conceptual verbosity must be reduced according to the fitness of intuition. This reduction is declared in the following passage:

“… we must rather practice ‘reflection,’ i.e. make these acts themselves, and their immanent meaning-content, our objects. When objects are intuited, thought of, theoretically pondered on, and thereby given to us as actualities in certain ontic modalities, we must direct our theoretical interest away from such objects, not posit them as realities as they appear or hold in the intentions of our acts. These acts, contrariwise, though hitherto not objective, must now be made objects of apprehension and theoretical assertion.”12

Meaning-content derives from conceptual confoundedness. Immanent meaning-content in the very act of cognition means an underlying function of language in knowledge. The question arises: with what necessity does a meaning-content accompany any act of cognition? With what extra-theoretical glimpse is one enlightened of its feasibility? Phenomenology is not meant to answer this question. In a rather cryptic way, the central difficulty is once again interpolated with respect to the function of signification. To engage in signification of these objectifications, is “to allow phenomenological relations to work upon them in full purity.”13 Such a working-upon raises the question of articulation. To turn away from the entity in any act in which the entity is de-posited is to prevent any proposition pertaining to the entity’s existence as it is found in the act from interfering in the auto-nomous and quasi-anonymous articulation of act-recollection ushered in by a reflective intention. This reflective intention is an imperative of nihilative vigilance that clears the empty play-space in and for which articulation is nurtured and fulfilled unto logical efficacy. The primary aim of this reflective intention is to cast light upon the Idea of knowledge, the general whatness of knowledge and its essential laws.

When referring to the meaning-content of an act of cognition, words were described as standing-for something. This standing-for is a function of indication. What is involved is what is found in the series of articulations in which the reflective recollection finds that certain words are embedded in the operation of ideational abstraction. That such a word is found to be in such an act means an inflection of its concept is involved in the operations of knowledge pertaining to the acts in which a self-relation is constituted. It is observed “that ‘standing for’ will not count as the meaning which characterizes the expression. For signs in the sense of indications do not express anything unless they happen to fulfil a significant as well as an indicative function.”14 Since every sign indicates something, what will be elaborated upon is the fulfilment of a significant function. Mere indications do not signify anything. Words, as a mere symbol, as a mere fact of referral to that which the word signifies, are lacking in the clarification of why they are implicated in any reflection upon any performed ideational abstraction. But when it is described how words, as symbolic standing-for, must be interpreted with respect to what they ‘truly’ stand for, this truth will always be towards a this rather than that – its sensitivity to this phenomenological state of affairs rather than that state of affairs. The question arises – who is the guarantor of this ‘true’ standing-for versus the ‘untrue’ standing-for? Is not the ‘true’ obtained via a decision of selection among ‘untruths?’ To recapitulate - first, phenomenology raises the question, here, of word-meanings and what they truly stand-for – expressing a law of knowledge. Standing-for is an indicative relation. Hence, this is a non-empirical indication. A hermeneutic that is adherent to logical laws must intervene in order to describe the performance of the present act of cognition – for example, what is given in intuition. Word-meanings already fulfill a significant and indicative role, on the basis of signs. But then there is an extra-essential ‘indication,’ as to what law they indicate within a given objectified act of cognition. Or, more aptly put – how the law directs these otherwise an-archic non-empirical indications towards a reflection of itself. As for right now, the present distinctions of indications are put for the sake of establishing that a sign has a meaning – that it is a word, that it has an intimated significance, that it is there in the intent to signify.

Indications are that in which a “belief in the reality of the one [state of affairs] as motivating a belief or surmise in the reality of the other [state of affairs].”15 It is rather clear that indications are made with reference to empirical existents, that their role in the fruition of ‘belief’ and ‘motivation’ is how they rather ingratiate knowledge to perform an act of drawing the boundaries of significance in which proper acts are to be performed. Such an evaluation of indication rather serves as the intermediary between two parts. One is concerned with the evaluation of indications as existing things. What is important is the distinction that is made between deliberate production and deliberate “artificial bringing-about.”16 Logical situations are pre-dated by a production, a natural technic, versus a bringing-about, an artificiality. With production it is not simply a matter of the indicative relation of standing-for. All signs stand for something. Rather – an existent that is produced to “serve as such indications,” as being in the place for, is a full sign “whether they exercise this function or not.”17 A pair of glasses, as suited for restoring eye-sight, will always indicate that someone has poor eyesight, regardless of whether they are worn by the owner or lying on a table. But a pair of glasses used by someone in the moment to point towards a thing is an artificial bringing-about. What is essential to recognize out of this example is that a ‘natural technic’ or production is a made entity that compensates for an essencelessness whose possibility of corruption is a constant possibility. Without this constant possibility of corruption, any deliberate bringing-about remains purely relegated to the contingent use of its standing-for. The designation of deliberateness extends to its essential unity: “a thing is only properly an indication if and where it in fact serves to indicate something to some thinking being.”18 As though this ‘where’ is unaffected by how the entity that is serving as an indication is constituted. For how is one to understand motivation as itself a unity of judgement when indications are relegated to a logical essence in the form of “this ‘since,’ … is the objective correlate of ‘motivation’ taken as a descriptively peculiar way of combining acts of judgement into a single act of judgement.”19 The ‘since’ is chosen selectively; it is a grammatical word as seized in the reflection of logical indication to gesture towards a unity of otherwise individual acts of existential cognizance, acts of judgement through indication of what is included in a state of affairs. Perhaps a deeper ‘indication’ lurks here, when it is further digressed that this since, as a way of unification otherwise individual propositions, operates in the association of ideas. It “operates creatively, and produces descriptive characters and forms of unity.”20 Despite the denunciation of a word as symbol, there is a symbolic characterization in the indicative association itself, as when A summons B into consciousness, vague characterizations are produced here to describe such an manifestation as ‘feel their connection forcing itself upon us.’ In fact, the serving-for of a produced indication upholds the “constant result of associative functioning… they no longer count separately, but help to present an object different form themselves.”21 Associative functioning seem to operate on a symbolic level, whose working back and forth are admitted as responsible for the creation of “additional phenomenological characters and unities” untethered from the empirical experience as such.22

If the ‘since’ is somehow the grammatical word that solicits a unity of acts of judgement, it is misleading. For phenomenological purposes, it amounts to “just this: that certain things may or must exist, since other things have been given.”23 Such a formulation seems to exhaust the totality of hypothetical reality on the basis of what is seen. However, these are not strict boundaries. In fact, can the bordering in which existents are included in the instantiation of any phenomenological state of affairs ever be truly closed? The invocation of ‘since’ is a mere appeal to a logical demand upon grammar that does not think into the combining of acts of judgement, the association of propositions. Encompassing this ‘since,’ guiding its directionality, is truly a look into the role of a hypothesized reality. For it is not just this: indications amply demonstrate that any such belief or motivation in a state of affairs calls into attention that: there exists that which cannot exist without (…). Before elucidating this necessary attachment, what is noticed in the grammatical instance of the ‘since’ is that it stands for not an entity, but a correlate of a motivation. It stands for a movement of knowledge. The ‘cannot exist without,’ then, is the collected backdrop around which a correlate of motivation is regionalized, is that through which the motivation wades through. Thus, it seems peculiarly rash when it is said that the motivation of unifying acts of judgement, or a collection of intuited propositions, itself has the character of a judgement or a proposition. For can there ever be a single ‘is’ for something that is not simply an act but an incessant activity? An autonomous one at that? Without the cannot-be-without, there is no motivation or association – there is no knowledge. The unity of judgement presumed to be of motivation is buttressed by an attachment that is non-propositional. We must come to grips, with all the gravity that this implies, that the live carrying-out of and within a phenomenological state of affairs also spurs the neither-alive-nor-dead carrying-out of this pre-phenomenological collection that the phenomenological state of affairs necessarily refers to. Unrealities exerting a force from a null-space that may never arrive. The Earth is just a three-dimensional playing-ground for these manifolds. And we humans refuse to unfold them for fears of being flattened.

Such pre-phenomenological drawing of boundaries depends on an ontological hypothetic. For the cannot-exist-without is just as binding as it is conjectural. Its gradual working-through entails the enrichment and/or impoverishment of the phenomenological state of affairs. Thus, that which is beyond the present phenomenological situation but must be brought in, means the gradual formation of a symbolism. Proper symbols do not just include or exclude an exteriorized entity from partaking in a phenomenological state of affairs. Through their forward and backwards movement, they entail that any phenomenological working-upon any state of affairs at once works upon the excluded, in a way that sensitive phenomenological contents can be exchanged without harming the sanctity of any state of affairs as this phenomenological situatedness. For (1) the same content can be subject to alternate intentional unities and (2) these unities are not delimited by a conceptual inherency. Thus, if mere signifying indication is what motivates a belief in the inclusion of existents in a single phenomenological state of affairs, then a symbolic scheme of indicatives ushers in the transversal deepening of a state of affairs being phenomenologically worked-through.

The aim of elucidating indication towards logical ends is to draw in entities, and in science, the collection of relevant propositions attained through acts of judgement upon given objectivities, culminating in a ‘pure logic,’ as the field out of which the sciences draws from. With expressions, it is not just about an inclusion of existents but a inclusion of possible meanings. Hence it refers back to the ideal possibilities of applying propositions that are already in a knowledge, and are already applicable. Such a presumption of the applicable has a logical intent, for at the moment it is about a reduction to the logical signification of any constituted objectivity. These distinctions will have two steps whose order of priority is not at all inconsequential: first, expressions in communication, and second, expressions in soliloquy.

The priority of communication is hard to avoid, for the sense or meaning of an expression is defined as the “certain sequence of mental states, associatively linked with the expression, which make it be the expression of something.”24 Hence, it is not so much about concrete meanings, but whether an association of mental acts and the sense made therein belongs to an indicated existent. Expressions have their unavoidable origination in communication. However, the reduction to pure logic must in a sense presuppose an interpretation of what this being-in-communication means. With the positing of expression as inextricably bound to a process of associative mental linkage, there is in some way a meaning-nexus that is hoisted into the interiority of a subject. What is common between this interpretation of communication and this interpretation of soliloquy is that they are both posed with respect to the priority to signify.

There must begin a first hermeneutic of the symbolic scheme of indicatives that establish communication and soliloquy prior to the logical description. To do so, there must be an attendance to the listeners.

For all that the logical reduction grasps is that there must be a mutuality. Such a declaration is frankly tautological. In order to tip these scales, or to inculcate the minimal a-logicism necessary for the reduction, a speaker ‘produces’ the expression; “he must endow it with a sense in certain acts of mind, a sense he desires to share with his auditors.”25 It is desire wherein one must lose himself for the sake of the logical in order to regain himself at the precipice of his expression’s exasperation. The invocation of production is doing much work here when it was seen that production refers to a serving-for in which the thing serves to stand-in-for an essencelessness whose possibility of corruption is constant. But is this serving-to-stand-in-for not the position of the listener? Is there not an essencelessness spurring in the speaker, that which would give him the desire? It will be then admitted that:

“He does this inasmuch as he takes the speaker to be a person, who is not merely uttering sounds but speaking to him, who is accompanying those sounds with certain sense-giving acts, which the sounds reveal to the hearer, or whose sense they seek to communicate to him. What first makes mental commerce possible, and turns connected speech into discourse, lies in the correlation among the corresponding physical and mental experiences of communicating persons which is effected by the physical side of speech. Speaking and hearing, intimation of mental states through speaking and reception thereof in hearing, are mutually correlated.”26

These are mainly pre-logical givens that are set to establish that there is indeed communication there. Reifying the signifying priority, it is this intimation that will see bare actuality in communication, and ideal possibility in soliloquy, with all that this ideality entails desirously. One, then, has to be felt as though one is being spoken to. Moreover, that who is spoken to is spoken to insofar as they are capable of speaking. This is not a material physicality of simply hearing word-combinations. It is an ideal physicality, a manifold. Thus, the effected physical side of speech can no longer simply refer to the sign as indication but symbol as indicative. There is a symbolic formation in the formulation of expressions. And these indicatives are not indications of existential belonging, such as the intimation function being a moment in which a sense is expressed to someone, but of meanings that can be attributed to and possibly experienced. There is this space, then, through the cannot-exist-without, that these unrealities spurred by the symbolic, are vied over in the life-and-death in communication. If done properly, communication can raise things from the dead. Such a tension can only be broached logically in the unexplained admittance that “mutual understanding demands a certain correlation among the mental acts mutually unfolded in intimation and in the receipt of such intimation, but not at all their exact resemblance.”27 In a way, there is no absolute closure for any phenomenological state of affairs until it has totally passed away. Perhaps even – that which it cannot-be -without has been made extinct. For the word ‘certain’ in all of these logical investigations are the indications of a product of a reflection.

In other words, ‘certain’ entails a phenomenological intervention whose meaning in the present instantiation is that some unity of acts of cognition persists here. But it must be pressed: the polarity between affirmation and denial of resemblance must be an enduring being-without-essence. Moreover, it must be an enduring being-without-essence with no final solution. What serves as a produced thing, a symbol, is only a temporary covering-over of a being-without-essence, one that allows communication to pass towards what has not yet been communicated. Internalizing this means there is no such thing as a stable phenomenological state of affairs. The logical is only a necessary response to the tearing-forth of this being-without-essence that communication crops up to amend. Symbols, then, must be formed, for otherwise there would be no making-up-for this tearing-forth. In fact, everything serves a symbolic purpose, since this enduring otherwise-than-being is without-death.

With this in mind, to transition into soliloquy is to first understand that nothing of any concrete meaning is involved here. An expression has a certain association of mental acts to it. The functional difference between expressions in communication and expressions in solitary life is that the latter is claimed to be without indication. “The existence of the sign neither ‘motivates’ the existence of the meaning, nor, properly expressed, our belief in the meaning’s existence.”28 The center of this re-establishes the logical situation that communication cannot ever guarantee: that to use as an indication, as a line along which a possible expression is made, is that it must be perceived. The locus of the situation is entrapped in the subject as that which primarily signifies. Hence, the logical retreat to soliloquy is the ultimate a-logicism: to bathe in the whisper of the meaning of the words that are alive, working upon the solitary soliloquist in his own self-incubating retreat. For what is the are expressions trying to do? They are trying to gesture towards a certain existence of associated mental acts. Expressions were framed originally as inextricably fulfilling its communicative function. Without the context of being-in-communication, there is no expression. Hence, when it is said that expressions still operate in soliloquy, there is a transversal expectation of some kind of signifying parallelism. It thus omits the soliloquy of the listener. Soliloquy-in-listening – hereby named the word-watcher – is the epitome of the relationship between knowledge, language, and the cognitive act. There is no expression without a listener in mind: the association and motivation of various cognitive acts, especially with respect to the intersubjective validity of scientific propositions, are tied with respect to the desire to address another.

Part of that which is aimed at in this investigation is the logical re-cognition of the fact that an expression has meaning. Expressions function in communication insofar as they are intimating. The intimating function of expressions are ultimately aimed towards the wider sense of “all acts that a hearer may introject into a speaker on the basis of what he says.”29 The threat of this introjection is an enduring, constant threat of essencelessness. But natural technics, production, is that which always stands in for the constant possibility of an essencelessness as a restoration from essencelessness.

We must understand now that when the listener is invoked in communication, the imagination is invoked in soliloquy to make up for that essencelessness that the listener, in a sense, counter-achieves. Introjection, for one, prevents there from the situation wherein the “hearer perceives the speaker as manifesting certain inner experiences, and to that extent he also perceives these experiences.”30 In this model, the hearer is rendered to a state of pure passivity. In fact, they are excluded from the interior life of these rendered mental experiences. “he does not, however, himself experience them, he has not an ‘inner’ but an ‘outer’ percept of them.”31 Such is the attitude that must be fostered if one is to have an objective glance of the necessary distance for a subject to perceive an entity in its objectness. That is after all the goal of these Logical Investigations. But what this move essentially does is nullify the potency of introjection. This is the ultimate a-logicism of logic. The speaker is rendered an unabating seat of adequate intuition – they have an unequivocal right to the totalizing act of conceiving their mental acts as totally their own. By contrast, the hearer is rendered an enduring seat of inadequacy. Nevertheless, the listener is in effect nullified by finding a surrogate in soliloquy. By the existence of the expression and its meaning, when our interest ceases at giving it meaning, there is the mere sound. When we however “live in the understanding of a word, it expresses something and the same thing, whether we address it to anyone or not.”32 But remember – expressions are in a propositional form. This is one of the guiding assumptions of knowledge. Thus, what we lost in a propositional expression to a listener, we reduce down to the word in soliloquy. But once again, the assumption of the subject as primarily signifying essentially generates this structure: that the elements of propositional thinking are lived wholly in me. The production of propositional thinking, the very restoration of the constant possibility of corruption, is always in the hands of the one who signifies. It is this very logical imposition that inculcates the stammer of submission, as though the model of a listener is one who is obediently silent. Or rather – that a listener who is silent after hearing his proposition is the ideal symbol of a conquered introjection, that the forces of evil potent in the listener have been quelled, that I am once again allowed to retreat and gorge on the fruits stuffed within my sanctity.

And this is seen in a very subtle moment, when the imagination of a ‘vocalized’ expression is encapsulated and ridden of its existential residue towards a listener. “The imagined verbal sound, or the imagined printed word, does not exist, only its imaginative presentation does so.”33 It is imagined, it is occurrent, but it has no existential weight. Yet why is this the primary imagination? Why is the so-called ‘flaw’ put ahead of a possession? Because it is a soliloquial re-enactment. That there is an imagination of the physical in the primary act of imaginative presentation, its denial of an existential status is a symbolic indicative, a symbolic act. To be given this element in an imagination is to show that in imagination there is a codification of the listener that in some sense speaks back through the imagistic intervention. Whomever is imagined as one’s own symbolic whisperer, is the figure around which motivation and association derives from. It is they-as-symbol, either in the flesh or in the mind, where entire logical unities are spun around and in fact spun around as objective, as if they rally the fantasies of men effectively enough. The imagination of the whisperer is not a signifying act. It precedes imagination insofar as it is this keyword, in its being-without-form, that spurs imagination, for fact of not being given in the flesh. It precedes imagination and enters into ones own mind from with and without. The soliloquy-in-listening can only usher in from a voice that is not totally ones own, a voice that in fact prevents there from what is being heard from entertaining any completeness. A potentiated voice that labors both with and without. In fact, an a-symbolic formlessness in which signs are transfigured into a word, as possible element of meaningful expression. Hence meaning is extrapolatable and circumfixal in the very instantiation of communication: the word scheme underlies this. When you are in the midst of expression, there is that introjective glare pointed not merely at your physical body, but in your head. And that listener you imagine and that listener they imagine, they speak between each other in keywords, codifying the relations between one another, ensuring that what is spoken has significance and that the proper intentions are grown to facilitate that essencelessness which forever surges outwards.

A subject that is primed to signify reaches the reduction of expressions themselves: “whether they occur in dialogue or soliloquy, two things seem to be left over: the expressions themselves, and what they express as their meaning or sense.”34 What had taken place in that discussion between communication and soliloquy is the parallelism of a signifying equivocation. Both the communication of propositions and the construction of the propositions are meant to exhaust the expression in its isolable objectness. Under the idea of Pure Logic, meaning is related to what is objective. When an expression is broken apart into its components, the question now beholds whether that meaning expresses a meaning that is obtainable from out of that object to which it is related to. Obtainability is the background through which the Idea of expression or meaning is up against. “But if the object is not intuitively before one, and so not before one as a named or meant object, mere meaning is all there is to it. If the originally empty meaning-intention is now fulfilled, the relation to an object is realized, the naming becomes an actual, conscious relation between name and object named.”35 There is a curious inversion that is at play here. That which was reduced by the establishment of the signifying function of the subject is now the authenticator of its meaning-intention in knowledge. There is a vacillation between these elementary manifolds and a logical, almost dialectical, upheaval whereby the signifying substratum of expressions are isolated and then brought up to be tested by the realm of knowledge. In a sense, the signifying function does not cease until a fulfillment is realized. The signifying function of the expression is a tear in the fabric of knowledge. Thus, when the fulfilling act is introduced, it “is not essential to the expression as such”36 because it is essential for the expression’s initiation as a component of knowledge: “these acts, which become fused with the meaning-conferring acts in the unity of knowledge or fulfillment, we call the meaning-fulfilling acts.”37 Such a ‘more or less’ is institutive of the meaning-fulfillment. The “empty intuition” is merely posed with respect to a relation to objects as things. It is merely posed with respect to obtainability as intuitability. However, with respect to the obtainability of meanings themselves as such those from complex state of affairs, there could be no ‘intuition.’ In fact, the ontological hypothetic of the cannot-be-without means that the meaning-intention a striving-for-fulfillment. Yet the meaning-fulfillment itself cannot be articulated with respect to the meaning-intention. Hence, there is already an introjective cutting-away-at the meaning-intention in the striving-for-fulfillment of the intention: the inexistence of the word-watcher – that very anonymous agent of en-codification and de-codification. And this fulfilling act appears as the act “expressed by the complete expression.”38 Such an expression would find completeness if it were felt beforehand its obtainability. That is why when we “yield ourselves to enacting the meaning-intention and its further fulfillment, our whole interest centres upon the object intended in our intention.”39 The flashing of this curious inversion strikes again, when isolating the elements of propositions by living in the sense of the imagined word, our direction now emphasizes the world. From word to world does knowledge compel us to learn through.

Otherwise, there is a loss of stamina here. Besides some various elaborations on the connection between meaning to ideal reference, there is reached a limit: “to characterize the various acts in which the relevant ideal unities are constituted, and to throw some light on the essence of their actual ‘coincidence’ in knowledge,” will not be achieved in the manner of expression. For expressions, “even when they function outside of knowledge must, as symbolic intentions, point to categorially formed unities.”40 This standpoint is the essential limitation of the logical reduction of knowledge. Then there is a distinction between expressions and meaningless expressions. Akin to the equivalence between knowledge and true knowledge, a meaninglessness expression is “no expression at all.”41 Nevertheless – why the introjection of a meaninglessness? It depends on what it is that meaning is identified with. Thus an expression in this area of the world may be meaningless for that area of the world. Nevertheless, since it is towards a common object, there is a necessity of oversignification. Oversignification must generate, through a symbolic modification of indicatives, that which allows the other speaker to speak forth – to give back the gift of speaking.

Keywords of the Generative Hermeneutic (II)

At this point the Logical Investigations is focused on a characterization of acts which confer meaning. The expression has been phenomenologically reduced to its components of meaning-intention and meaning-fulfilment. Related to this is the notion of obtainability. Obtainability designates a kind of elementary field. To intend a meaning is to already invoke that that meaning is obtainable, that it is here or there. Insofar as it is here or there, it can be fulfilled with respect to this here or there. Thus obtainability designates a field of possible fulfillment. There is a characterization of the conferment of an elementary meaning-fulfillment in the elementary field of intuitable meanings. One conferment of meaning is that it must be understood.

However, it will soon become apparent that what is desired is how the one-and-only meaning-intention must mutate in order to attain different modes of meaning-fulfillment. In a series of subtle moves, the gradual retrieval of the meaning-intent, the reduction of its phenomenological import, for one of these modes inevitably jettisons it towards another mode. These moves eventually culminate in the notion of apperception and the limits of the experienceable – to go towards the obtainable, one has to at once move back into the past, to reactivate a past. In the hands of the fields of fulfillment, in the temporal contractions between a willful future and a hopeful past.

~

First, phenomenology takes aim at the imagistic and psychologistic presumption that for every understood meaning there is accompanied a mental image of the object that the meaning relates to. Here we are faced with the object-relation that a meaning is related to. Insofar as this must be an arousal, the field of communication remains operative here – the onus of the conferment is on the listener. Characterizing this act according to such a modality therefore entails re-directing of interest, away from any constitutive necessity being put in the hands of the one that is not conferring the meaning. For the purposes of Pure Logic, the mental image of the listener must not be seen as necessary for a word to have meaning. “A comparison of a few casually observed imaginative accompaniments will soon show how vastly they vary while the meanings of words stay constant.”42 The analysis will continue to individual word-meanings themselves but the full “understanding of words” which “persists after such an image has vanished, and cannot therefore consist in its presence.”43 The word-meaning is not coterminous with the momentariness of its apparent imaginative fulfillment.

For one – it is fascinating that Husserl is, through the route of objection and address, in some way is mimicking the field of communication and, through the retraction of conferring prime necessity upon fulfillment, reduces the field of communication to a phenomenological interiority of consciousness. Thus, the purpose of isolating these acts according to their phenomenological bent is coterminous with an unintentional reduction to the self-consciousness of that communicative manifold. By interpreting knowledge with respect to the self-evident of logical laws and accordingly reducing at each moment the act therein, the manifold of consciousness as phenomenological self-consciousness is not merely discovered but traversed and instituted through the very initiation into the phenomenological objectification of acts of cognition. The later explications of transcendental subjectivity necessarily follow from the reduction of obtainability to fully self-evident positing. The manifold of transcendental subjectivity pertaining to phenomenology in its traced-out maturation is the theoretical manifold unfolded for phenomenological theory.

One such objection that Husserl clings to is the matter of observability. It is clear that on the basis of describing meaning-conferment, such an objection is pointless. But, what must be caught onto is the modes of fulfillment as types of obtainability. Obtainability as observability in the context of the investigation is the false equivocation of observability of listener’s image to the observability of the speaker’s word-meaning. Hence “we do not at all think that, where symbols are understood without the aid of accompanying images, the mere symbol alone is present: we think rather that an understanding, a peculiar act-experience relating to the expression, is present, that it shines through the expression, that it lends it meaning and thereby a relation to objects.”44 Symbolism is finally invoked, although simply as the carrier of ones intended meaning, as a cipher of a signifying meaning-intent. An object is a symbol when meaning is conferred upon it in the expression, when one indicative is under the striving-for-fulfillment. Concern towards word-meanings entails an object relation to observability as intuitability. But “it is this sense-giving act-character – which differs entirely according as our interest plays on the sensible sign or the object presented through it, with or without representative imagery – that meaning consists.”45 Such articulations as interest and play signify the solicitation of symbolic indicatives. Meaning is maneuverable according to the fantasization of indicatives. Fantasizing desires converting an object over to a symbol as an act of carrying ones intention. Interest generated by this fantasy must therefore entail an excess, an extra-intentional driving-into. This entails a positional obtainability, a correspondence of desired meaning and its relation to an intuitable object that can fulfill the meaning. But the phenomenologist must step-back further. He would like to play in this sphere of meaning-variation and keep it to himself, as thought without intuition.

Under these constraints, expressions must function ‘significantly’ “without illustrative intuition.”46 But here, senselessness is remarked as having meaning as such, as an expression that refers to “what cannot be objectively put together.”47 Here we see the first concrete, material instance of an ideal, cognitive reduction. The cannot-be-without as its non-propositional boundary of significance, entails a putting-together. It, as an ontological hypothetical, is a putting-together that is objective. Here there is the collection of possible propositions. But behind this all one can imagine monstrous lurking amalgamations shunned or entertained according to a field of significant constriction. We must think of the uncommunicable, the unobservable. Essentially, the cannot-be-without as objective putting-togetherness is not solely constituted by the subject. There are elements which position the subjects in multifarious ways, elements which even posit who is in this communication or that communication. There can be subjects who are positioned in both this and that field of ‘communication,’ and can hence communicate to one something from the other that would be unobtainable by the prior. Yet this meaning will pass through the field of communication. Its meaning can be incorporated. Hence we have a keyword: meaning-incorporation.

And hence “if the meaningful is not to be found in intuition, speech without intuition need not be speech deprived of thought. If intuition lapses, an act like that which otherwise hangs about intuition, and perhaps mediates the knowledge of its object, continues to cling to the sense-giving expression.”48 The transition into communication is constricting into soliloquy. And hence the formless soliloquy-in-listening will be the word-watcher, the en-codification and de-codification as surrogate of a sign-symbolic comprehension. It picks out the word elements that are incorporated into a collection of running parallel expressions. Such a surrogative word-watching manifests in a ‘games-sense.’ A created, playful manifold of meanings with relations to incorporated objects/signs. “For mere signs, in the sense of physical signs bereft of all meaning, do duty for the same signs alive with arithmetical meaning: it is rather that signs taken in a certain operation or games-sense do duty for the same signs in full arithmetical meaningfulness.”49 This duty, for logical purposes, is as a stepping-stone into its intellectual, symbolic relevance – but for a game.

The game is a manifold. And it is clear that understanding without intuition pertains to a sign’s surrogacy as en-codification. The game encodes the sign as sign. It can be used as a sign anywhere, but when incorporated within a game, or theoretical manifold, it is a stepping-stone. And it is in these games that meanings that are intended in the game are without intuition. The meaning does not relate to the sign as such but the way in which the sign is dematerialized. As per the first imagination incorporating an image, the symbolic act is a dematerialization, a choice of whether to incorporate or not. And hence:

“…purely symbolic meaning-intentions often do not clearly keep themselves apart, and do not permit of the easy, sure distinctions and identifications which are needed for practically useful judgements, even if these are not self-evident… But where meanings shade unbrokenly into one another, and unnoticed shifts blur boundaries needed for firm judgement, intuitive illustration naturally promotes lucidity.”50

This appears like a ‘general’ rule. But it unravels something deeper: the dynamics of the Logos. Understanding without intuition brings one to the fact that a sense of meanings as senseless can be understood, such that the senselessness, as without intuition, is understood. In confronting this there was the idea of senselessness as a cannot-be-without, as a cannot-be-(objectively-put-together). Remaining in the realm of propositions, putting-together is now associated with the elemental keeping-themselves-apart. Putting-together and taking-apart are the dynamics of the Logos. With respect to the Idea of Pure Logic, and thus the phenomenological reduction of this Idea, putting-together pertains to the practically useful judgements as intending an expression while the taking-apart pertains to the symbolic meaning-intentions. One imbues the sign with an intent, converting it into a symbol whose meaning derives from a game. Its effectivity would then be experiencing the game. Which coincides with the invocation of apperception. Now it is a manner of “experience sense-complex, … interpretation derived from experience.”51 Interpretation guides the analogical appropriation of sign-elements, treating them as building-material. It is upon this building-material that, in an act that first lifts the material up, it is the very being-able-to-be-lifted that all such intentions have come to admit, added on. Hence obtainability is a non-propositional structure of experience as such. In fact, it delineates, as unfolding the logical manifold, which of the objects in that manifold are ‘to be experienced.’ The experience of obtainability as the experience is the establishment of a functionality, which can only be accomplished by the fulfillment. The meaning-intention cannot on its own procure the logical manifold pertaining to the phenomenological state of affairs it desires to functions in. In fact, the totality of possible functions depends on a primordial activity on the side of fulfillment. When an intention strives-for-fulfillment, initiating this striving-for releases fulfillment to a re-collection, an a-temporal un-winding of the significant logical manifold pertaining to the phenomenological state of affairs that the function functions in. Yet by the ontological hypothetic of the cannot-be-without, the playing-out of the cannot-be-without transfigures the fulfillment’s apperceptive unfolding. Pertaining to one instance of the unfolding request may not pertain to the next instance of the unfolding request for the same phenomenological state of affairs.

There can be situations in which good intentions are added to an image, converting it to a symbol of, perhaps, love, that is introjected into the intellect of the other. And it is the other who, under the ruse of a specter, through the a-symbolic enactment of meaning-incorporation, denies that image due to its pompous ambivalence and hence severs, perhaps permanently, the chance of experiencing a love that was meant to be.

And it will be the hubris of this specter whose slow, aching deterioration, depending on the technological fabrications that are deployed to delay the inevitable, and since it never really belonged to him, will culminate in the playing-out of a fantasy that simulates holding onto a hand he banished himself from, and this imageless unreality will be perpetually suspended before his eyes like a marionette doll, taunting him of an eternity unlived, until the day of his last word, where that very same doll will watch if he, the murderer of her love, has the guts to whisper her name into the alibi of his passing breath.

Every phenomenological intention, once fulfilled, instigates an apperceptive unfolding of the theoretical manifold. Now it is understood that the objections that are raised, as indices of obtainability, are what preliminary arise, in the primary imagination, whose symbolic enactment clears the way for the logical reduction of the act as it is in that manifold. From the standpoint of self-evidence, the theoretical manifold is cleared away on the basis of its reductive steps.

But at this point the exploration will experience a disturbance. The fluctuation of meanings themselves and the investigation into the ideality of unities of meaning will reach a point where the fluctuations of manifolds are no longer permitting a straightforward reduction of acts. The emphasis will be turned towards expressions, as carriers of meaning, being split by an “unavoidable rather than chance ambiguity.”52 It comes from how our meanings vary when alongside with persons and experiences.

What is important to recollect here is how the phenomenological securing of these cognitive acts will follow (1) a reduction to mineness as intuitive presentation through (2) a description of this reduction according to (3) the self-evidence of logic as pure Idea. It is this theoretical penchant for self-evidence that makes phenomenology nevertheless an interpretation. This very penchant forecloses phenomenology from a proper treatment of intersubjectivity. Hence, there are undercurrents whereby communication begins to attract things that are otherwise not belonging to it in its original instance, such as obtainability delimiting the limits of what is able to be experienced. As an example, for any genuine meaning-intention as striving-towards-fulfillment there must be the pure experiencing of obtainability. The pure movement of obtainability, since it ushers in from an exteriority that is outside the experiencing of present intuition, is a non-propositional extra-experiential boundary-making. Obtainability – whether as intuitability, observability, so on and so forth i.e. being-intuitable, being-observable – is navigating a manifold. When it is internalized that these movements are away from the theoretical attitude of self-evidence, then this movement is precisely the precursor that begins to dismantle self-evidence as ideal hypothetic, disturbing the sanctity of its concept as such.

~

Hence the following is an invention of phenomenology for the sake of incorporating meanings in communication for oneself, that is devised to “promote clearness” amidst the ineliminable ambiguity of obtainability: “we shall define the following distinction between essentially subjective and occasional expressions, on the one hand, and objective expressions, on the other.”53

Objective expressions manifest a meaning that can be understood “without necessarily directing one’s attention to the person uttering it.”54 Subjective expressions, on the other hand, are far more elaborate. They “belong to a conceptually unified group of possible meanings, in whose case it is essential to orient actual meaning to the occasion, the speaker, and the situation.”55 Preliminarily, phenomenology tries to invoke the flesh in order to banish an empirical movement from the realm of ideality. But it will soon be exposed that this is merely the sensuous analogue of a non-empirical redirecting. With respect to the manifold of communication: objective expressions are meanings that are enduring. Subjective expressions, on the other hand, enact meaning-incorporation. It encompasses the totality of members within an instance of communication: occasion, speaker, situation. The conceptual unity entails a pre-expressive stratum of expressions that are contained in the instance of communication. It necessitates a pre-comprehension of obtainability. By beginning to express a meaning that involves these obtainable elements is to enchant the communicative manifold with its meaning, rendering it objective.

Keeping to the content of expressions, the personal pronoun ‘I’ renders any expression with a lack of objective sense. “It is the universal semantic function of the word ‘I’ to designate whoever is speaking, but the notion through which we express this function is not the notion immediately constitutive of its meaning.”56 With respect to expressions in solitary speech, they are realized in an immediate idea of personality. It is a universally operating indication of an ownmost idea of personality. Yet the “word ‘I” has not itself directly the power to arouse the specific I-presentation… in its case, rather, an indicative function mediates, crying as it were, to the hearer ‘Your vis-à-vis intends himself.”57 In any intent to present oneself, ‘I’ does not invoke anything. It must be ‘I’ and something else. To present oneself is to express that something has this meaning for oneself. It is an indicative function that is “exercised for the other, singular presentation, and, by subsumption, makes the latter’s object known as what is here and now meant.”58 There will be particular importance that is placed on the here. For it relates to subjective expressions – “the meaning of ‘here’ is in part universal and conceptual, inasmuch as it always names a place as such, but to this universal element the direct place-presentation attaches, varying from case to case. In the given circumstances of speech, it acquires heightened intelligibility by subsumption under the conceptual indicating presentation of ‘here.’”59

The inventions of objective and subjective expressions are made in order to render clarity to the ambiguity of communicative manifolds. It remains restricted to the first-person descriptive, but now the authentication of its expressions as objective are totally subjugated to a reference towards other subjects, situations, events, etc. What will be attempted here is a retrogressive involution of these symbolic indicatives back into the subject in order to expose an a-priori element whose authority lies in disrupting the substantiality of subjectivity.

~

Consider the following lines related to the concept of the ‘here:’

“To use the word is to refer to one’s place on the basis of an intuitive, believing presentation of one’s own person and location…the genuine meaning of the word is first constituted in the variable presentation of this place … the meaning of the ‘here’ is in part universal and conceptual, inasmuch as it always names a place as such, but to this universal element the direct place-presentation attaches… of speech, it acquires heightened intelligibility by subsumption under the conceptual indicating presentation of ‘here.’”60

The communicative manifold, as the basic and perpetually open manifold, positions the entities that are wound up in its grasp. The place of each and every subject is based upon believing presentations of personality and location. Communicative manifolds, then, institute a positionality. Place, as the unity of personality and location, is the authentication of positional variation. Positionality is therefore the positional scheme pertaining to all entities in an instance of communication. ‘I’ am a ‘me’ with respect to the positionality. On the basis of positionality, all other entities in communication is not the ‘here’ of ‘me.’ They are there. Hence, all that is in communication is there-for-me-to-signify. Furthermore, even if an entity is not intelligible for me, it is ‘I’ that can discern its indeterminateness for myself. My personality is a conceptual confoundedness united in the ‘I’ whose positional pointing-over-there through expression raises the particular positioned thing into not just an object relation but a self-relation with an incipient conceptual confoundedness, an incipient unity of meaning. As meaning-incorporation, the heightening of intelligibility is essentially a pointing-over-to where an association of ideas is taking place. Unnecessary for the current elaborations, this taking-place fissures the indicatives attached to the positions-as-symbols. By the being-there of that which is not me, and in having to turn towards that ‘here’ in which a unity of meaning is addressed, the inclusion of the ‘I’ in any expression, unavoidable because of the first-person ‘I think;’

“…lacks an objective sense… it is the universal semantic function of the word ‘I’ to designate whoever is speaking, but the notion through which we express this function is not the notion immediately constitutive of its meaning… but since each person, in speaking himself, says ‘I,’ the word has the character of a universally operative indication of this fact … an indicative function mediates, crying as it were, to the hearer ‘Your vis-à-vis intends himself…’ we should not suppose that the immediate presentation of the speaker sums up the entire meaning of the word ‘I...’ undoubtedly the idea of self-reference, as well as an implied pointing to the individual idea of the speaker, also belong, after a certain fashion, to the word’s meaning… The one [self-reference], relating to the word’s general function, is so connected with the word that its indicative function can be exercised once something is actually presented: this indicative function [particular idea] is, in its turn, exercised for the other, singular presentation, and, by subsumption, makes the latter’s object known as what is here and now meant.”61

Taken in isolation, any expression which includes the ‘I’ will never have a full objective sense. Nevertheless, the objective senselessness inculcated by this personal pronoun is reducible through an interpretation of its positional, subject-bound determination. When the ‘I’ speaks of their thinking of something, there is a co-and-pre-experiential discovery of conceptual obtainability. What is thought of is there, ‘I’ am here, my fellow communicators are ‘there.’ That towards which my ‘I think’ goes or ‘is,’ can be indicated to all that is ‘here’ in this manifold of communication. The positional difference between me and my ‘I think,’ that my ‘I think’ never exactly coincides with my here-as-such, is the reason why the immediate presentation of me as speaker never catches up to the presence of the ‘I.’ This is the straying-away. The ‘I think’ as what-I-am-thinking, is always oriented towards a there, a there in which ‘thinking’ is de-posited. Thus, the ‘I think’ is de-posited into the locus of the instance of communication as such. That which ‘I am thinking’ is to the behest of the communicative sway. So on and so forth, until something is actually presented i.e. made objective. And hence, when it is required that;

“we shall call an expression objective if it pins down its meaning merely by its manifest, auditory pattern, and can be understood without necessarily directing ones attention to the person uttering it, or to the circumstances of the utterance…. we call an expression essentially subjective and occasional, or, more briefly, essentially occasional, if it belongs to a [1] conceptually unified group [2] of possible meanings, in whose case it is [3] essential to orient actual meaning to the occasion, the speaker and the situation…”62

Subjective expressions are first classed as belonging to a conceptually unified group. Such a conceptual unity relates directly back to the conceptual universality of the ‘here.’ With the ‘here,’ intelligibility is a conceptual obtainability which can be brutishly conglomerated as the totality of there-for-me-to-signify of each and every ‘I’ in communication. Every subjective expression therefore lies in a there that has not been raised to a here. What remains hindsighted when isolating the ‘subjective expression’ to an expression that is essentially i.e. cannot-be-without empirical re-orientation is that this empirical re-orientation is induced by the universal and a-conceptual here-there dyad. It is a-conceptual, insofar as the there covers over a potential ingredient of conceptual confoundedness. Hence, if objective expressions are expressions that can be understood without turning toward any ‘there’ that is not the ‘here’ of the speaker or the listeners, then it is objective expressions that are relative to a communicative manifold because they are expressions in which all in communication can signify. Objective expressions are subjective expressions that are there-for-all-to-signify. Hence a subjective expression is put from out of communication into communication if that which is there-for-me-to-signify is made here-for-all. This is named enchantment. Excommunicative meaning-incorporation, on the other hand, is disenchantment. Since objective expressions are expressions that have no need to orient towards the situation or speaker, they truly have no need to even be expressed. Thus, all expressions are ‘subjective.’ But – akin to how the ‘I’ is not just its indicative function, there are subjective expressions within the bounds made by objective expressions and there are subjective expressions without the bounds made by objective expressions. Subjective expressions being-discussed-about within the bounds of communicative manifold enchants the communicators. Their back-and-forth crisscrossing through the indicatives pertaining to a symbolic positionality generates ‘objective’ expressions. An objective expression is generated and builds up the boundary of communication when a meaning that has been made here-for-all-to-signify has been reduced to a meaning that is here-for-all. Yet when there is a generated, expressed objective expression, there is also a breakdown of the objective expressive boundary. The breakdown disenchants other subjective expressions that are in the sway of being entertained. This buildup and breakdown, this with and without, of the objective intelligible boundary, is trans-temporal and a-temporal. The manifold of communication can be re-invoked when the originating subjects are in its manifold. To the extent that it is being-in-communication, is when the membrane can be actively built up and broken down. The manifold of communication can be used as a fact when the subjects are not in communication.

And all of this is only possible because even before the subject begins to utter, there is already a straying-away-from-myself towards where my thinking is involved and as outside the communicative manifold taking place without me. And insofar as the ‘here’ designates the position of my ‘I’ as my substantiated personality, this element that enchants my thinking is named the straying-away-from-here.

The straying-away-from-here, as preceding the ability to signify and hence subjectivity, rounds out the substantiated personality of the ‘I.’ That in soliloquy one is always listening and hence in communication with oneself, our thinking is carried away from our here by this straying-away-from-here. Not as a mere coming-into-knowledge with a self-relation, but of a practical insufficiency. Listening-in-soliloquy exposes ourselves to a fundamental insufficiency that is with and without the broader encompassing world. Hence, by enabling one to breakup new manifolds, such as the I-with-the-world or our I-without-the-world, everything is established as though it is a communicative manifold. Hence, the logical opposition of interior/exterior does not exist in eternity. It is produced by an act of thought and hence is only true for the span of presence is endures for. The a-logical with-and-without continues to weave. Hence, genuine thinking is the act of thought that is spurred by the straying-away-from-here. The a-logical ‘I,’ is the I that is here, and joined to it is an I ‘to be’ that is straying-away-from-here. When straying-away-from-here, ‘it’ pulls the substantial I towards the not-here of its cannot-be-without. As with and without the ‘I,’ through its cannot-be-without, the straying-away-from-here necessarily draws the boundaries of the phenomenal here of the ‘I.’ This is its sphere of experience, as sphere of possible phenomena and indicative phenomenalizing. Hence, insofar as each and every member in an instance of communication orients themselves in a way that will never exactly correspond, we have that each member’s standpoint is rather the empty center of an a-logical scheme. To illustrate an operation between this I-here and this straying-away-from-I, for instance; when coming across something or someone, the ‘I’ that is straying-away-from-here may fissure the sphere of experience by breaking a striving-for-fulfillment. This break occurs via an enchantment with and incorporation of an object that would have otherwise lied beyond the sphere of experience as it was instantiated when intending the indicative and being imbued in the phenomena that this indicative facilitates. Hence, when subjective expressions carry meanings between the boundary, that is named transmission. And since they necessarily pass through each and every subject’s sphere of experience, the meaning is a translation.

It would perhaps not be so rash to establish that the communicative manifold is how the basic scheme operates. Since the straying-away-from-here splits the ‘I’ with-and-without the world, the scheme is properly a-logical.

This does not run into conflict with what follows as the phenomenological elucidation of meaning variations. In fact, it converts the onus of phenomenological description. What Husserl worries about is whether the “fluctuations of meaning are enough to shake our conception of meanings as ideal unities or to restrict its generality significantly.”63 Eventually this comes down to the variation of meanings themselves, as meanings fixed and meanings changeable on occasion. This restriction eventually boils down to the interiority of the signifying subject, where objective meanings are fully out there while others are “live submerged in the flux of subjective mental experiences…”64 Such is impossible, since whether ‘subjective’ or ‘objective’ the meaning is an ideal unit whose unequivocal relation to its object is simply varied by the subject. In fact, “each subjective expression is replaceable by an objective expression which will preserve the identity of each momentary meaning-intention.”65

Furthermore, phenomenology concedes to the failure, generated no doubt by the pervasiveness of its artificial dichotomization of expressions, that this replacement cannot “be carried out at all, will, in fact, never be so capable.”66 The “unbounded range of objective reason” renders the phenomenologist impotent, as “infinitely removed from this ideal.”67 In eternity there is no change of meanings. All ‘change in meaning’ is simply the change in the act of meaning.

It must be therefore declared – the dichotomization of subject-object, epistemology-symbol, that reaches its apex in the forced dichotomization of subjective and objective expressions, must be superseded by the infinitely-nth-dimensional integration of a-logical schemes.

Meaning is a unit of ideal unity. Their objects are states of affairs, whether identified as singular intelligibility or the collection of propositions that govern scientific judgement. States of affairs are occasions of conceptual confoundedness. Meanings, as units, are composed of meaning-intentions that objectify in adherence to the meaning pointed out. Such a composition is not arbitrarily founded in the collection of pieces themselves but in the way they are configured. Yet the very manner of configuration as a live meaning is barred off from the scientific commitment to self-evidence. Unless the communicative manifold incorporates their subjectivity, phenomenology cannot ask: why and how does this occasion occasion? It will always have to give itself over to the metaphysical speculation of conceptuality and the political maneuvering of conceptual confoundedness. Hence all propositions are fated by the phenomenologist “ultimately point back to concepts.”68 The role of phenomenology – the very Idea of Phenomenology - in the movement of the concept is the cultivation of conceptual fitness and rigour. It spans across the ‘ideal fabric of meanings,’ it is an infinite discourse. The phenomenologist is tasked with uncovering the raw expressions of engendered objective expressions in metaphysics, the actually performed acts of cognition that deepen metaphysical work.

The elementary basis of a-logical schemes is the communicative manifold. Our ‘I’ that is straying-away-from-here draws the boundaries of our sphere of experience. As a communicative scheme, transmission occurs through schemes of experience. Translation is transmission that passes through one’s own sphere of experience. Elements that are transmitted through one’s own scheme of experience undergo a translation that is fit for the substantiality of the ‘I’ as it at that moment stands.

Now that the investigation has transitioned over to the phenomenological content of experiences of meaning, there warrants a brief interpretation of how meanings, as ideal units, are living in schemes.

What a meaning ‘is,’ its content as an intended unity, is “nothing which could, in a real sense, count as part of our act of understanding,.”69 Meanings are expressed. In the metaphorical sense of the word, expressions give to sight, they press out from a horizon, an understanding of its being-there. But – “we have also shown that there is something in the correlated acts which really corresponds to such selfsameness of meaning.”70 Hence the components of these meaning-intentions which are able to be reflected upon are really the description of a live, schematical operations as to how a meaning stands in the communicative manifold. The phenomenological implication of this is all understood expressions, as here-for-all, have the same meaning-intentions as an invariant character. As replaceable, subjective expressions are built upon so as to bring forth objective meaning. There are partitions of significance according to how many schemes are involved. A meaning that is here-for-all is here for this all.

The grammatical meaning of ‘all’ would connote a collection of individual entities, with emphasis on the individuality of each and every individual. ‘An all’ is a non-identifiable, a-grammatical cannot-be-without. By grammar, For an expression to live as here-for-all, the ‘all’ that is there when the meaning that is there-for-all is expressed becomes a meaning that lives in that communicative manifold between each and every I that is in that All. Thus at the basis of any and all instantiation of ‘an All’ is the One. Meanings live in communicative manifolds insofar as the meanings cannot-be-without all ‘I’s’ that catch sight of the meaning. Hence there is a difference between the Eye and the I. The Eye is that which always keeps watch of the straying-away-from-here. The straying-away-from-here draws the boundaries of one’s sphere of experience. Obtainability, as the becoming-obtainable of intuition, intelligibility, observation, is a straying-away-from-here that the Eye always sets on. By addition, the ‘I,’ as substantial personality, is imbued in a phenomenal indicative within their sphere of experience. Depending on who is becoming-obtainable in the straying-away-from-here, the Eye, akin to the word-watcher, manifests as the singular Eye of an All. The Eye of an All – such as: insiders, outsiders, friends, enemies – has a distinct look. One can feel the gaze of the Eye as one’s meaning is incorporated by the All. There is an incorporation that the Eye preserves the sanctity of. The catching-a-glimpse-of that which is becoming-obtainable enchants the entity, incorporating it through transmission and translation.

As the imagined listener, the Eye encodes and decodes communicative manifolds as an instance of this One All, keeping track of keywords and thus preserving the way in which meanings are enchanted and articulated therein.

The Quintessence of the Generative Hermeneutic

This point of the exploration calls for urgent exploration. It is concerned with the trajectory of the exploration’s projected course. Without proper clarification, the explorations risk the loss of their incisiveness.

From here on I will make reference to explicit sections of the Logical Investigations while continuing to labor alongside their published order.

~

Investigation I, §29 - §35: this is where the quintessential juncture opens up. Between this span Husserl establishes how the scientific study of ideal unities that are meanings are subsumed under the rubric of logic. He will then go on to discuss how the essences of these ideal unities are experienced. What is striking about this stretch is the closure of the relationship between meanings and the expressions and experiences of them as legitimized through intuitive, or simple, givenness.

§29 establishes the precedent through which the phenomenological treatment of meanings is justified. That logic, insofar as it deals with propositions, concepts, judgements and so on, inextricably deals with ideal unities called meanings. Moreover, for any ideal unity that is a meaning, the disambiguation from sensible residue such as psychological or grammatical connections, followed by the intelligible adequacy of its unity to its objective correlates, ensures that singular essences of meanings remain within the realm of pure logic. Hence there are two manners of separation:

“It is clear, secondly, when we note that the theoretic content and content of a science is no more than the meaning-content of theoretical statements, disembarrassed by all contingent thinkers and occasions of judgement, and that such statements are given unity by the theory’s pattern, which in its turn acquires objective validity through the ideally guaranteed adequacy of its unified meaning to the objective correlate meant by it (which is ‘given’ to us in self-evident knowledge). Undeniably what we call ‘meaning’ in this sense covers only ideal unities, expressed through manifold expressions, and thought of in manifold act-experiences, but none the less clearly separable from such chance expressions and from such chance experiences of thinking subjects.”71

When justifying the procedure of simplifying ideal unities that are meanings, the first appeal is made to that upon which ideal meanings rest – the theoretic content of a science. As logic, they pertain to scientific thought in general, the theoretic content and connections of any science whatsoever. Hence what ought to be disambiguated is to setup a scientific comportment, pertaining to Pure Logic, that secures the sanctity of its own internal constitution. The above therefore introduces two metaphysical parallelisms. One is involved with ‘statements’ and their theoretical appropriation. The other is involved with the manner in which ideal unities that are meanings are found in this theoretical appropriation and are pointed out via expression and act-experiences. Just when singular ideal meanings are scrubbed of their grammatical and psychological associations, and then afterwards are verified of their singular intelligible adequacy, now it is singular statements, whose logical adequacies are securely selfsame throughout any and all instances of the factual collecting of them, that are then tightly woven into a singular unity of meaning. What applied to the intelligibility of a singular ideal unity of meaning as objective adequacy is correlated to the theoretical meaning-content as objective validity. Thus, on the assumption of self-evident knowledge, the unity of meaning pertaining to a theoretical content contains individual meanings whose expressions or act-experiences of them are not consequential to the intelligibility of these meanings, to their essence. Such a methodological separation is apparent on account of how a unity of meaning as theoretic content is given unity by a pattern while the individual meanings are apprehended through an act-experience of them. Thus, upon apprehension by a subject the singular meanings all stand in a fixed relation. What is given is what is given.

What, then, does any intention have to intend? The statements that are given unity by a pattern cannot be ever exhausted by the individual meanings that arise from out of this unity. To secure the adequacy of individual statements, and then to collect these individual adequacies into a single unity of meaning, never coincides with the validity of this unity of meaning. This is because propositions “are themselves the elements of inferences.”72 Inferences are the elements behind the “unified contents, syllogisms, i.e. the self-identical meanings of certain complex statements.”73 They are ultimately “an ideal relation among possible statement-meanings, among propositions.”74 The existence of these ideal relations are matters of subsistence “without essential relation to an empirical thinker.”75 It is by no means argued here that there is an essential relation to empirical thinkers. Such a proposal is a bastardization. But unlike the objective relations pertaining to the adequacy of ideal meanings correlated to propositions, these ideal relations among propositions are potentiated betwixt the objective relations of ideal meanings taken in their individual adequacy. And hence to go along a potentiated inference, to actualize that ideal relation, is not a difference that is ever constituted by the empirical thinker, but is nevertheless an ideal that is traversed by thought. Since these are relations between concepts and propositions, and logical meanings pertain to concepts and propositions, it must be accessed by an act of thinking that grasps the meaning of the concept or propositions it finds itself tied in. This is nothing other than reflection.

§34 introduces reflection. It may be important to note that Husserl begins explicating the act of reflection with respect to meanings of individual features of intended objects. Regardless, reflection begins with the idea that;

“If we make a statement, we judge about the thing it concerns, and not about the statement’s meaning, about the judgement in the logical sense. This latter first becomes objective to us in a reflex act of thought, in which we not only look back on the statement just made, but carry out the abstraction (the Ideation) demanded. What is characteristic of such thought is the context of theory, and the theoretical consideration of the latter, which is carried out in step-by-step reflections on the contents of the thought-acts just performed.”76

Subtle but systematically detrimental: the reflective act is potentiated and appended to a meaning intention as a way in which thought can think. The possibility of Ideation is always founded upon a meaning-intent – only upon the back of an intention can there be an Ideation, and only upon the connected instances of Ideation are theoretic contents explored. And if the logical content of each and every judgement can be disembarrassed from the judger, then by analogy the meanings found in a unity of theoretic content can be separated from expressions or act-experiences of them. Husserl thus makes the jump from reflection upon an individual judgement to reflection upon a unified thought content. This unity of meaning is treated as a singular objectivity, and hence self-samely apprehensible as an ideal propositional meaning – as an object intended by a subject. The basis of this simple intentional givenness, however, seems to shake when such a reflection upon a theoretic-content…

“… sets in at later stages, and an identical propositional meaning is continuously meant in it, idealized and identified in our unified thought-context, and thought of as one and the same. The same is the case wherever a unified theoretical demonstration is being wound up. We could utter no ‘therefore’ unless there was also a glance at the meaning-content of the premises. In judging the premises, we not merely live in our judgements, but reflect on their contents: only by glancing back at these does the conclusion appear ‘motivated.’”77

Reflection opens up three movements that do not coincide with the apprehension of ideal propositional meanings: the looking-back, the carrying-out, and the step-by-step. When reflecting upon the meaning of a statement, the looking-back at the statement just made renders the carrying out of Ideation trivial – it identifies a singular feature. But then Husserl makes a leap of methodological naivety by translating the triviality of this carrying-out to the manifold due to the invariance of intentionality as the givenness of meaning as originating out of a subject’s intention of an object, as though the manner in which the ideal meaning of individual propositions are drawn is identical, in intentional essence, to how one can draw the ideal unity of a manifold. While the reflection upon a statement about an individual feature is simply an abstraction to a singular species, the reflection upon a proposition as found in a thought-context is carried-out through the thought-context. Moreover, this thought-context, even when considered as a singular unity of meaning, is situated among other singular unities of meaning that are not intended, yet are nevertheless ran up against. Husserl, in trying to maintain the grasp of a manifold as pertaining to intended objects, extrapolates the selfsameness of the manifold via the selfsame identical meanings of each and every proposition underlying it therein, but this will never exhaust the theoretical apprehension because enunciating the impossibility of inference without the glance at their meaning-content only guarantees conclusions of a particular instance of the manifold. When in a manifold, the metaphor of looking-back is not even apt, for it is not a matter of retrieving a previously instantiated object but of a looking-around, of where to go.78 Such where-to-go is never ascertained through a givens of simple intuition but rather in the cognition of the reflex-act that Ideation first unveils as already motivating even the simple collection of statements to bind into a unity of meaning. When the motivation of any conclusion is revealed only at each intentional step, one must never begin to locate and isolate the essence of a meaning in this intentional step, for such piecemeal component-wise abstraction only elaborates on the what and how but never the why. Taking this question seriously involves understanding how looking-back and the step-by-step sets up a passage whose traversal mediates the contents of what is being-given as the carrying-out.

Jump back to §29. When it is stated that statements are given unity by the theory’s pattern, the vocabulary of an acquisition is invoked precisely ‘in turn’ from out of the intention of a patterned imposition. But the moments in which givenness is invoked can be equiprimordially replaced by the language of acquisition. Rather than a theory’s pattern giving unity to a collection of statements as instantiating an instance of theoretic content, the collection of statements rather acquires unity through a theory’s pattern. This formulation permits one to disambiguate the potentiality of a statement as it can be through many patterns. The unity of meaning pertaining to this collection of statements hence acquires objective validity through ideally guaranteed adequacy of a unified meaning to an objective correlate meant by it. Such a statement is already articulated through the language of the through. This means that theoretic validity is made out to be established on the basis of the adequacies of their objective correlates. Hence these meanings remain selfsame as expressed through manifold expressions. Yet can the same be asserted for act-experiences? When referring to theoretical validity, its partitioning into the adequacies of individual meaning-statements are confounded by a givenness in self-evident knowledge. But such a statement can only be defended when based on the methodological underpinning of exhausting the essence of meanings in their meaning-intentions. This is corroborated when the givenness in self-evident knowledge is paralleled by the givenness in act-experiences. Expressions of an individual meaning are always given in an associated act-experience, statements unified in an individual unity of meaning-content are always given in an associated knowledge operation. The trouble that opens up is that by this very isomorphic duplicity of in-ness, of the parallel analogization of concrete intentional act-experiences to the hypothesis of knowledge as a collection of intentional act-experiences, of the sensibilization of expressions to the intelligibility of their founding acts of thinking, only the fact of correspondence between subjective thought-connections to an objective unity of meaning can be identified as there while the state of the correspondent identity as such cannot ever be broached. For…

“What, however, he thinks of, and what he knits together in unity, are concepts and propositions together with their objective relations. An objective unity of meaning, i.e. one adequate to the objectivity which is self-evidently ‘given,’ thereby corresponds to his subjective thought-connections: this is whatever it is, whether anyone realizes this in thought or not.”79

What Husserl says here is that the operations implicit in a particular theoretical treatment of a collection of statements are knitted relations between the Ideated propositional meaning-contents. Hence phenomenology only establishes the elements of object-wise meaning that serve as the basis of a manifold, that tie a manifold to an explicit representable objectivity. And what about this possibility of this representation? By the abiding character of the correspondence, it is a divulging looking-back that, unlike the step-by-step, does not blink. The separability of meanings from their expressions only acquires meaning through this possibility of representation, and this possibility of representation must precede or attach to any apprehension of manifold relations. Only reflection, then, is authorized to make modifications to manifolds that intentionally-based reflection will only catch a glimpse of – ‘it is whatever it is.’ This admittance must not be confused with reflection as such, as though the realization of correspondence itself has no carrying-out, looking-back, or step-by-step, as though it were a simple givens of an identity. Although the cosmological-demiurgical experience of subjectivity seems to lurk underneath – as in, the totality of objective components at the disposal of an ideal tinkering – such an example exposes that the identity of correspondence between unity of meaning (objective relations as elements) and thought-connections (as subjective tinkering with objective relations) for that type of subjectivity is rather one of empty free-play. That ‘same’ manifold institutes a different identity of correspondence for other types of subjectivity, but at once the relation of a subjectivity to the ‘same’ manifold renders it no longer selfsame. The act of Ideation alters the manifold upon its catching-sight-of-it. Ideation of any such correspondence is a creative act – even basic intentional reflection modifies things that are not intended. Hence there is no absolute identity for the correspondence between unities of meaning and thought-connections of them. And if the realization of this correspondence is the inception of the possibility of intending any act-experience of its corresponding unity of meaning, then while the meanings found therein are conditionally separable from the expressions and act-experiences of them, the manifolds that bear those meanings are absolutely inseparable from the History of Ideation.

To intimate what it is that Ideation travels through, to intimate that meanings, soon to be debarred from a methodological requirement, lie upon a field of subsistence upon which relations that first germinate from the intellect can take place, what must be evaluated is the role of the concept. The textual position of propositions pertaining to the pertinence of the concept is noted down in §29. The scientist is described as someone primarily concerned with “concepts, which are for him ideal unities of meaning, and also in the truths, which themselves are made up out of such concepts.”80 The qualification of the concept as an ideal unity of meaning is misleading because it obfuscates the ideational process of concept formation. Nevertheless, the predominance of the concept is still asserted here alongside how propositional content “ultimately point back to concepts.”81 Following along one is immediately arched back to the inference, the passageway that draws conclusions. If propositional content are ultimately composed of concepts, and propositions are the elements of inferences, then concepts must make way for possible ways of intending inferential acts. Such a making must not be associated with how the scientific investigator “does not make the objective validity of thoughts and thought-connections… but that he sees them, discovers them.”82 In the act of discovering them, the essence of meanings are found in this “self-identical intentional unity.”83 Self-identical, that is, while wading in the consistency offered over by that meaning in the span of its instance. For when the “genuine identity that we here assert is none other than the identity of the species,”84 the connection between the object directed towards and the meaning intended therein is transferred to a realm that operates on the basis of ideal relations as mediated by the species identified with. But by this very identifying-with there are unintended meaning-predicates that attach to the meaning-intention of an inferential act spearheaded by the species. And despite any attempts to purify this seemingly “logical” assertion of the species – for instance, the self-assertion of the identity of the species as assertion of the identity of my species, which commits phenomenology to the rightfully ascertained yet misguided accusations of subjectivism and also commits phenomenology to the lurking possibility of philosophically rationalizing the construction of elaborate political machinations – of its political undertones, what peculiarly follows is a confirmation, both comforting and discomforting, that the ideality of the species is not a normative ideal. It is an infinite task: “The ideality of what is specific is, contrariwise, the complete opposite of reality or individuality; it represents no end of possible endeavor, its ideality lies in a ‘unity in multiplicity.’”85 And this infinite abyss is felt in the absolute non-coinciding of meaning and concept. The concept that is a component of a thought-of proposition by no means exhausts the meaning of the concept invoked in the very act of meaning: “The universality that we think of, does not therefore resolve itself into the universality of the meanings in which we think of it.”86 Meanings in themselves, then, is a misnomer, when the very expression of them resolves itself in a novelty wherein “wherever a new concept is formed, we see how a meaning becomes realized that was previously unrealized.”87 And hence, at the very conclusion of the first Investigation, even after expression is exclaimed to only discover, even after the relation between meaning and expression is closed, one kneels to the admittance that…

“There are therefore countless meanings which, in the common, relational sense, are merely possible ones, since they are never expressed, and since they can, owing to the limits of man’s cognitive powers, never be expressed.”88

~

What has been passed through is just a route that communicates the a-priori institutional impotence of meaning-intention nested in the very articulation of its scientific commitment. Yet phenomenology nevertheless strikes upon a crucial truth: reality is meant to be penetrated. Reality can only be known to the extent that it is penetrated, and by those constituted to penetrate it. In fact;

“Battling against a seductive notion, we laid it down that the essence of expression lies in a meaning-intention, and not in the more or less perfect, more or less close or remote, illustration that accompanies of fulfills that intention. If, however, such illustrations are present, they will be intimately fused with the meaning-intention.”89

Rigorous, scientific logic must battle against the seduction of emptily pontificating the magnanimity of itself – especially to the face of its potential authenticators. For an intention does not enjoy a real satisfaction without the acquisition of an object that does not already belong to oneself. The subject-object divide, the key separation of modern philosophy, is implicated here. Everything must begin with a divide between the subject and the object because a consciousness of being-divided as being-separated-from that which one desires is the precursor to any true intention and to the genesis of any intentional structure in which an object is a member of its inferences. Only the event of a true acquisition, and under the glance of a proper listener, is the structure – or unity of intention – bridging the gap between subject-object is constructed. And only on the negative basis, on the entropic degeneration or passing-away of the subsistent field that these constructions stand, whether once before or to come, is there deconstruction.

Can one still hold onto the notion that the essence of a meaning lies in the meaning-intention? To secure the results of a rigorous science, a meaningful notion. To investigate the paramount organizations that render such sciences as meaningful – a meaningless notion. When Husserl introduces reflection in the context of Investigation I it is made with respect to an individual feature of an object. On paper it appears that such a designation is made in order to remain scientifically cogent. Abstraction at this point has nothing else to point to but the identity of the species that this individual feature falls under as a singular object. But there is a deeper meaning. Such a methodology is injurious to the very object that serves as the basis for the formulation of a meaningful proposition – the concept. The scientific starting point of phenomenology begins with an object that ‘is there.’ Such an observation that ‘there is this’ must have already ideated its concept. It is not even a matter of pre-predicative experience. It is about already being in the eccentricity of labyrinth-like thought-connections and already being pre-meditated by these thought-connections. Our very existence, taken individually, is preceded by a conception such that to be conceived is at once to whisper the keyword ‘there is this’ and to furthermore append this keyword to the ‘this,’ whatever it may be, in order to communicate that it ‘is there.’ The purpose of this non-propositional keyword is to keep open the apprehensibility of that ‘this’ for potential Ideation, which if ideated runs down to its very haecceity. This keyword is a primeval element that is necessary for the articulation of an orderability that ensues when a meaning-intention of any object is even conceived. Meaning-intention thus depends upon a potentiated form of the very arrangement that it finds meaning in. The institution of this potentiated unreality, such as the validity of a theoretic content, is thus spurred through the meaningfulness of the ideal unities that are meanings, which hold onto the object. In turn, the object is in-finitized by the limitlessness of the meaning to the extent that it gives normative limits adequate to the meaning in its enjoyment. To first posit reflection, then, alongside the individual species of the object is to primordially apprehend that the institution of theoretical content, its institution-as-validation, is spurred by a historical intention that takes up those cradled by its possibility. And the Ideation of such objects is their concept. As acquired by intentional structures, objects are in-finitized to the extent that their concepts tolerate displacement, guided by the very species that cling onto them, guided through unknowing with the hopes that it endures as one. And hence, if there are expressions that cannot be expressed due to the limits of man’s cognitive powers, the expansion of the limits of the man’s cognitive power responds to the man’s history of realizations as genuine acquirer of meanings that never belonged to him prior.

If the essence of an expression lies in a meaning-intention, if the meaning-intention is an act-experience correlated to a meaning that belongs to an object, if in the act of intending an object one is not conscious of the meaning, if reflection is the act in which the meaning of an intention becomes conscious, if reflection is the act in which the motivation of the intention appears, if the meaning-intention of any object is hence motivated by meanings that are not the meaning of the object that the meaning-intention intends, if the essence of expression is the discovery of meanings belonging to objects, if thus the meanings that motivate the meaning-intention are meanings that are not discovered; then there is a meaning that attaches to objects prior to any intention, that is generated by neither subject nor object.

Furthermore – if expressions discover meanings that belong to an object, if possible meaning-intentions are intentions that can be expressed, if the power to express is the power to articulate, if a meaning-intention that can be expressed is one that is already articulated, if articulation therefore collects the meanings that motivate the meaning-intention but are not discovered in this meaning-intention; if reflection intends through this articulation; then the meaning that attaches to objects prior to any intention, that is generated by neither subject nor object, is articulable.

Whatever this meaning is, must be formulated. The formulation of this meaning must utilize the resources acquired in this exploration.

~

A meaning-intention aims at an object with the intention of expressing a meaning that is of that object. Reflection is an act that, on the basis of a meaning-intention, intends the meaning as such of the object that the meaning-intention has aimed at. To comport towards any meaning, a subject must already be straying-away-from-here. But what is reflection? Reflection intends a meaning upon a meaning-intention that has been. In reflection, the meaning that reflection intends appears with respect to a motivation. A motivation is that which spurs a meaning-intention to step in this way versus that way. In stepping here versus there, the subject decides. In this decision, the subject brings the object from here to there, and hence brings along these meanings versus those other meanings that would have been brought along if the subject decided to step in a different direction. In reflecting upon this decision, there is a possibility that the subject may realize that something has been left behind. The subject has strayed-away-from that which is not there in his meaning-intention, but must be there. Reflecting upon this intention gives the meaning of that which has been left-behind. Yet this straying-away-from-here is towards that which has been left behind, which is out-of-sight. The straying-away-from-here, however, strays away towards that which is there in the possibilities of the meaning-intention, and hence knows that it is there. Hence, when the decision is made, the straying-away-from-here refuses the subject of its object that it has left behind, but brings back the meaning of this being-left-behind and scolds the subject that in its intention a meaning was left-behind. Thus, the straying-away-from-here has its own wishes. But these wishes are only guaranteed to be spun for a consciousness in which no necessary meaning is left behind. This is none other than transcendental consciousness, whose sole actuality is God.

Yet the phrase ‘no necessary meaning is left behind’ is articulated through two keywords: cannot-be-without and incorporation.

Intentionality is therefore encoded by the following keywords: straying-away-from-here towards that which cannot-be-without-incorporation.

With such a code, one can begin to introject: why can this not be without incorporation? Why is this straying-away?

Not only so. The fact of this articulation distorts the transcendental necessity that warrants the phenomenological hypostatization of the intentional-correlation. That from the Logical Investigations phenomenology is lead to a transcendental idealism is the result of its circumscription of categorial forms in terms of meanings whose identity is that of the ideal species which necessarily inhere to objectivity. Through step-by-step meaning-intentions that gradually undresses objectivity of all earthly clothing, that gradually denudes objectivity of all heavenly inadequacies, the build-up of ideal meaning-possibilities culminate in a quintessential Ideation that strikes when objectivity, catching a glimpse of itself, shirks-back and leaves, out in the open, the very organ responsible for an intent now dispossessed of any and all previous worldly significance. This is none other than the intentional-correlate itself. Only from this point onwards does a transcendental phenomenological philosophy begin and slowly maturate this dug-up power of consciousness. And the methodological hypostatization of its necessity for philosophical inquiry ensures that the possibility of fulfillment in subjectivity is held onto without relinquishment and plumbed for its infinite gravidity.

But it is man, feeble feeble man, who can only do it from time to time, step-by-step. That which makes this power arise is never his alone.

The articulation demonstrates this. The true intention begins at the moment when the cannot-be-without-incorporation is passed through in thought. Only then is an intention honed that will be given that which it originally intends to acquire. Yet this intention begins precisely when the straying-away ends. And this is on the assumption that the straying-away will collect the meanings that thought knows is without incorporation. Yet straying-away-from-here does not have to do so. Straying-away-from-here need not follow the order, since the straying-away-from-here is a conjunction that is articulated before any and all intention, and hence the intentionality of transcendental consciousness. And it is this very conjunction that must be disarticulated if man is to collect the objects, arrange them in an orderly manner, and propose in a way – as in, state a logical pro-position – that expresses whether or not that very disarticulated unity of meaning is truly and simply there, and proposed to that very straying for whom a Yes means they begin again, or a No means they refuse.

But does not the possibility of reflection itself rest in the recognition that there is always something that will be left behind? A looking-back knowing that certain meanings must not be taken along in the step-forward? An irresolvable wish of completeness that generates our moralities, that generates our politics, that generates our art, that generates our sciences, that generates our existence?

The codification of intentionality, ‘straying-away-from-here towards that which cannot-be-without-incorporation,’ opens up the a-logical generative hermeneutic. Phenomenology cannot simply hypostatize the intentional-correlate of transcendental consciousness as such despite its necessity for the apprehension of ideal meanings inhering to objectivity. Its power arises when the possibility of fulfillment is opened up to it by that which does not originate from its will alone. The essence of any meaning pertaining to objectivity is never sufficiently exhausted by intention alone. What is given in a meaning-intention – a shade of the concept, a consequence, a conclusion – is really an acquisition by raw thought, acquired through a sifting by raw thought, and passed over to a human subject by raw thought, and in a way that is never condensed nor fully exhausted by any intention besides the idealized one, an intention whose possibility is always posited but never actualized.

Philosophy gravitates around the possibility of this ideal intention. Its most prominent names have only deepened the theory and obscured the practice of this ideal intention. But this has been apprehended - there is no absolute correspondence between unities of meaning and their thought-connections. At the same time, there is an absolute inseparability between unities of meaning and the History of Ideations.

Any and all essence is momentarily definite, eternally transient. This does not repudiate philosophy. Essence lives in the flesh. And its bloodstream flows through the fingers of the most unsuspecting of us.
